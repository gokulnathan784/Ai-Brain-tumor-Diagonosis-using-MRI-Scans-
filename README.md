The proposed Brain Tumor Classification System using MRI Image Analysis with Xception and Grad-CAM successfully demonstrates how artificial intelligence and deep transfer learning can assist in the early and accurate diagnosis of life-threatening neurological conditions. By utilizing automated MRI image analysis, the system eliminates the need for manual feature extraction and subjective clinical interpretation, providing a fast and reliable computer-aided diagnostic solution for medical professionals.
Through the integration of the pretrained Xception architecture and specialized medical preprocessing (CLAHE), the system effectively extracts both local structural features and complex textural representations from brain MRI scans. This deep feature learning strategy enhances the discriminative capability of the model, enabling accurate multi-class categorization of MRI images into Glioma, Meningioma, No Tumor, and Pituitary tumor categories, directly overcoming the limitations of previous binary classification systems.
Experimental results demonstrate a high validation accuracy of >95%, strong generalization ability, and minimal misclassification, proving the effectiveness of the depthwise separable convolutions used in the Xception backbone. The implementation of a user-friendly Gradio analysis interface (NeuroScan Pro) further enhances the practical usability of the system by allowing healthcare professionals to upload MRI scans and obtain instant predictions with confidence scores. Crucially, the integration of Explainable AI (Grad-CAM) provides transparent visual interpretations via Thermal/Jet Spectrums, highlighting the specific pathological regions responsible for the predictions.
Overall, the system achieves its primary objectives of automated diagnosis, high multi-class accuracy, and real-time interpretable prediction. This research highlights the significant potential of deep learning-based medical image analysis in supporting early brain tumor detection and assisting clinicians in making informed decisions, thereby improving patient care and treatment planning.
